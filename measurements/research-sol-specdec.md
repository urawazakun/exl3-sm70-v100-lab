# Sol research brief: speculative decoding in the fixed V100 / EXL3 regime

25 September 2026. This extends `RESEARCH-prior-art-specdec.md`; it corrects claims there where later primary evidence changed the interpretation. **Labels:** `measured` = a published experiment or the operator's fixed observations; `vendor claim` = a checkpoint/runtime author's unreplicated statement; `inference` = transfer to our regime or an evidence gap. The operator's numbers are supplied in the brief and have no public URL. Every external factual claim below links to its source. A speedup on another GPU, model, quantizer, or workload is **not** a forecast for our V100.

## Ranked: do this first

## 1. Abliteration mismatch and cheapest re-alignment

| Evidence | Answer for our decision |
|---|---|
| `measured` — Gemma 4 26B-A4B abliterated: stock assistant acceptance by speculative position 65.6/43.3/29.2/20.5%. Retrained EAGLE-3 was reported at 84.4/74.9/74.1/72.7%, but **the columns used chat versus raw `/v1/completions`**. Raw prompts collapsed into easy repetition. Its original “~2×” throughput comparison is invalid for production chat. Paired chat: retrained ~46, pure body ~40, stock MTP k=4 ~53 tok/s. [Round 1 erratum](https://ai-muninn.com/en/blog/dgx-spark-eagle3-finetune-abliterated-round1) | `inference` — Abliteration mismatch is plausible, but the widely repeated “20.5%→72.7%, ~2×” does **not** establish a causal recovery of that size on matched chat. Correct the prior sweep accordingly. |
| `measured` — Same author's second run added 30k regenerated Chinese instructions to 50k English; 41h training. Chat EN/ZH 45/29 tok/s versus first retrain 46/27, and stock MTP 53/45. [paired Round 2](https://ai-muninn.com/en/blog/dgx-spark-eagle3-round2-null-result) | `inference` — More data did not repair the small head's acceptance ceiling there; one fine-tune is not automatically the answer for our DFlash2 gap. |

[pipeline](https://ai-muninn.com/en/blog/dgx-spark-eagle3-finetune-abliterated-round1) | `inference` — This is the **smallest documented abliterated-target head adaptation with hardware/time numbers found**, not a proven minimum. The 35h total includes regeneration and differs from 27B dense/V100. No defensible GPU-hour quote for Qwen3.8-27B DFlash2/DSpark is public. |
| `measured` — EDA adapts drafts to Qwen2.5-7B domain fine-tunes with 68k samples/domain, 20 epochs, batch 16; half-size target-generated selection. On medical tasks at T=0, base draft acceptance length 2.15 / speedup 1.39×, LoRA 3.79 / 2.36×, EDA 4.21 / 2.66×. The paper does not give a comparable GPU-hour figure or demonstrate abliteration/27B. [EDA experiment and appendix](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.09527) | `inference` — Independent corroboration that *fine-tuned target distribution* matters. It is not a cheap 27B training recipe. |

This is an absence-of-evidence finding, not proof none exists. [turboderp catalog](https://huggingface.co/turboderp), [huihui catalog](https://huggingface.co/huihui-ai) |

## 2. Quantized / instruction-bound target: does speculation pay?

For a fixed draft length, the relevant measured quantities are accepted tokens per round and **round latency**, including drafting, target verification, rollback, and KV work. The break-even condition is `mean committed × draft-off latency > whole speculative round latency`; acceptance alone is insufficient. This is an `inference` from the standard time-ratio formulation and our fixed timings. [SpecMQuant §3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22179)

| Evidence | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| `measured` — Controlled FP16/W8A8/W4A16/W4A8 EAGLE-2 comparison: 4-bit reduces relative speculative gain; W4A16 8B on A100 at 60-node verification tree has `Tverify/Tdecode≈1.8` vs <1.2 in FP16/W8A8; smaller trees improve W4A16 speed. There is an exception for W4A16 8B on RTX 3090 where EAGLE-2 adds no gain. [SpecMQuant](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22179) | `inference` — Strong evidence for **4-bit tree verification** becoming too expensive. It does not prove a monotone 4→3 bpw penalty for sequential MTP on EXL3 trellis; that measurement is missing. |
| `measured` — RTX 3090 Qwen3.6-35B-A3B Q4_K_M, single user: 138.9 tok/s no-spec, DFlash best 77.0 (k=8), 74.9 (k=4), 65.8 (k=16). Different MoE target and engine. The same author's later matched vLLM MTP k=1 test **won** by 27.5%; the earlier blanket hardware conclusion was retracted. [experiment and errata](https://hackmd.io/ODXuOQNzSiyUITz7g9mtBw) | `inference` — Negative llama.cpp DFlash result is real for that setup, but cannot close MTP or dense EXL3. Its suggested hidden-state quantization mismatch is a hypothesis, not isolated by an A/B. |
| `measured` — Qwen3.8-27B IQ4_XS, RTX 5090, patched llama.cpp: DFlash2/MTP/DSpark acceptance 4.80/4.28/3.62 tokens per cycle; 8-row verifier optimum; changing MMVQ→MMQ verify dispatch yielded +11%. [full campaign](https://github.com/LukasParke/qwen38-27b-5090) | `inference` — A 4-bit target can benefit if the verifier kernel is suitable. The verifier dispatch can matter as much as drafter choice. No EXL3 3-bit V100 extrapolation is justified. |
| `measured (operator)` — MTP k=3 34.77 tok/s, 1.62× draft-off; k=5/7 19.95/26.87; DFlash2 stock acceptance 0.446. Operator brief has no public URL. | `inference` — On *our* target, speculation already pays, and extra depth currently fails. The causal contribution of ALU issue, acceptance, and recurrent/KV verification requires separate timing; public work does not report our `acceptance × verification-cost` frontier. |

## 3. Training-free / lookup at long context and upstream status

| Method and availability | Measured acceptance length / limits |
|---|---|
| `measured (code provenance)` — PLD-style `ngram-simple`, `ngram-map-k`, `ngram-map-k4v` are in **upstream llama.cpp**, merged via [PR #18471](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/18471) on 2026-01-28; see [current upstream docs](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/docs/speculative.md). `ngram-cache` is also upstream, based on [#5479](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/5479), [#6828](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/6828), [#6848](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/6848). | `measured (paper)` — OWL's LongSpecBench averages (Llama 8B/70B) PLD 2.75/2.24, Suffix 3.41/2.61, SAMD 3.18/2.48, Token Recycling 3.16/2.97, EAGLE3 1.28/1.35, HOWL 6.14/5.31. A separately retrained 32k-window EAGLE3-L recovered to **3.23**, narrowing the gap; OWL remained 4.00. These are **benchmark averages**, not separate 32k, 64k, 128k cells. [OWL Tables 1–2 and 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.07535) |
| `inference (code check)` — Suffix Decoding, SAMD, Token Recycling, and HOWL are **not selectable upstream llama.cpp spec types** as of the checked [upstream enum/docs](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/docs/speculative.md). They are distinct published research methods: [Suffix](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.04975), [SAM-Decoding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.10666), [Token Recycling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08696), [OWL/HOWL](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.07535). | `inference` — `ngram-simple` approximates prompt lookup; it is **not** the same implementation or measured speed as suffix automata/Token Recycling. OWL supports training-free superiority on its benchmark, not a universal 32k–128k claim for Qwen/EXL3. Its long EAGLE3 control was limited to 32k training by OOM. [OWL setup](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.07535) |
| `measured (code provenance)` — `-lcs` in llama.cpp means `--lookup-cache-static FNAME`, a **static cache file**, not “longest common subsequence.” `ngram-cache -lcs` is upstream; see [server options](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/tools/server/README.md). | `measured` — Upstream issue [#27852](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/issues/27852) reports cross-request stale `ngram-cache`: fresh 41.51 tok/s and 903/1052 accepted versus second-request 19.41 and 73/660; suggested reset restored 42.37. This is a caveat for multi-turn tests, not a 128k acceptance benchmark. |
| `measured (operator, offline)` — n=4,m=8 copy-heavy match 98%, mean committed 8.98; prose match 20%, committed 2.9. No public URL (operator brief). | `inference` — No public source found with **per-length acceptance at 32k, 64k, and 128k for all named methods versus a trained head on the same target/hardware**. A direct table would require new measurement; claiming it from OWL's aggregate would fabricate precision. |

## 4. Depth `k` versus context for our 0.6–0.8 acceptance regime

These are **test priorities**, not measured optima at every context. `k` means sequential draft tokens, not tree nodes; acceptance rate alone cannot map k to throughput. [SpecMQuant cost model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22179)

| Context | Recommended k to test/use now | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| ~6k | **3**, compare 2 | `measured (operator)` 0.786 acceptance and k=3 is the only demonstrated winner. `inference` k=2 is a cheap comparison; no external EXL3 V100 sweep. |
| ~24k | **3**, compare 2 | `measured (operator)` acceptance 0.672, k=3 aggregate best; k=5/7 worse. `measured (external)` Q4 3090 DFlash 4/8/16 had shallow optimum k=8, but another architecture. [3090 sweep](https://hackmd.io/ODXuOQNzSiyUITz7g9mtBw) |
| ~71k | **2–3 A/B; retain 3 until paired result** | `measured (operator)` acceptance 0.588 and decode 19.6 tok/s. `inference` shorter speculation may reduce rejected verification work; no k-by-context measurement here. [quantized verification study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22179) |
| ~128k | **2–3 A/B plus draft-off and lookup; no published optimum** | `inference` from falling operator acceptance and rising KV cost. `measured (external)` OWL's long-context EAGLE3 slowed to 0.81× while PLD/Suffix were 1.59×/2.18× on Llama-70B, but it does not report our k sweep. [OWL Table 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.07535) |

`measured (external)` A separate quantized Qwen3.8-27B 5090 campaign found 8 verify rows optimal, whereas the 3090 Q4 MoE DFlash sweep peaked at 8 drafts, and the operator peaks at 3. Their conflicting optima demonstrate dependence on verifier/target/workload. [5090 campaign](https://github.com/LukasParke/qwen38-27b-5090), [3090 sweep](https://hackmd.io/ODXuOQNzSiyUITz7g9mtBw). `measured (external)` SparseSpec-L's A40/Transformers LongBench v2 study reports MagicDec-SnapKV k=2 at 1.74× and k=16 at 0.36×; its own fixed k=4/8/12/16/20 speedups were 2.10/2.39/2.69/2.66/2.62× versus adaptive 2.79×. It caps context at 64k. These are **not** single-user V100 EXL3 results. [SparseSpec-L tables and setup](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.27735). The original [MagicDec paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.11049) evaluates batches 32–256 on eight A100s, so its headline gain also cannot set our k.

## 5. What can beat speculation on old GPUs at long context?

| Candidate | Measured effect and quality status for our fixed target |
|---|---|
| Exact attention kernel / verify dispatch | `measured (external)` Volta fork q8-KV attention kernel ~1.88× at 101k and ~2.05× at 260k **for that kernel**, plus +27.8% cached-prefix prefill; Qwen3.8-27B 5090 verify dispatch +11% end-to-end. `inference` — exact arithmetic/path changes can be quality-neutral if bitwise/greedy output is checked; neither figure establishes EXL3/V100 end-to-end gain. [Volta fork](https://github.com/jackjusko/jusko-llama-volta-qwen3flash/blob/main/README-FORK.md), [verify campaign](https://github.com/LukasParke/qwen38-27b-5090) |
| Q8/FP8/low-bit KV | `measured (external)` the same Volta fork finds q8 KV 27.3 versus f16 28.7 tok/s at 20k; a separate V100 measurement reports q4 cache saves ~30% of total per-token memory versus q8, less than bit-width suggests. `inference` — quantized KV changes numerical output and is **not quality-neutral** without a target-specific quality test. It may buy capacity, not decode speed. [Volta fork](https://github.com/jackjusko/jusko-llama-volta-qwen3flash/blob/main/README-FORK.md), [V100 kit](https://github.com/andrewleech/v100-llm-kit/blob/main/docs/benchmarks.md) |
| KV compression/eviction/streaming | `inference` — dropping or approximating stored K/V is not quality-neutral for a 128k full-attention target. Paged allocation and exact KV reuse can preserve the model distribution, but no public measured single-V100 EXL3-27B speedup or quality-neutral 128k result was found. [vAttention exact paging study on newer GPUs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.04437), [H2O eviction paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14048) |
| Volta fork specificity | `measured (code provenance)` `jackjusko/jusko-llama-volta-qwen3flash` is based on `mistrjirka/llama.cpp` `ac8d815` and targets **2× 32GB V100 Qwen3.8-Flash-Next**, with much of its extra work on QSA/PLE/MoE; our target is dense 27B EXL3 on **one 16GB** card. The q8 attention sub-kernel is the transferable idea, not its whole benchmark. [fork README](https://github.com/jackjusko/jusko-llama-volta-qwen3flash/blob/main/README-FORK.md) |

| Closed or weak path | Measurement and scope |
|---|---|
| Deeper stock MTP here | `measured (operator)` k=5 19.95 and k=7 26.87 versus k=3 34.77 tok/s; closed under current kernel/drafter/context mix. No public URL (operator brief). |
| Stock non-abliterated DFlash2 as an assumed replacement | `measured (operator)` acceptance 0.446 vs MTP 0.683. `inference` — acceptance alone does not prove throughput loss, but it gives no reason to displace the measured MTP reference. No public URL (operator brief). |
| “One retrain gives 2× chat” | `measured` — endpoint-confounded claim corrected to ~46 vs ~40 draft-off and below stock MTP ~53; another 41h/30k samples gave 45 vs 53. | More draft tokens without cheaper verification | `measured` — 4-bit EAGLE-2 verifier ratio rises to 1.8 at large trees; 3090 Q4 DFlash k=16 65.8 vs k=8 77.0 tok/s. [SpecMQuant](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22179), [3090 sweep](https://hackmd.io/ODXuOQNzSiyUITz7g9mtBw) |
| Universal n-gram acceleration | `measured (operator)` prose lookup match only 20%. `measured (external)` Qwen3.8-27B 5090 ngram→DFlash2 cascades cost −8% to −64%; Volta fork calls n-grams on unique text a negative result. | “Q8 KV is faster because smaller” | `measured` — Volta fork at 20k: q8 27.3 vs f16 28.7 tok/s. It may still be required for capacity. [Volta fork](https://github.com/jackjusko/jusko-llama-volta-qwen3flash/blob/main/README-FORK.md) |
| Transplanting 3090 MoE or 2×V100 Flash-Next results directly | `inference` — model, quantizer, GPU count, and verification implementations differ; the 3090 author explicitly retracted a hardware-general claim after matched vLLM MTP won +27.5%. [3090 errata](https://hackmd.io/ODXuOQNzSiyUITz7g9mtBw), [Volta fork setup](https://github.com/jackjusko/jusko-llama-volta-qwen3flash/blob/main/README-FORK.md) |

**Unanswered publicly:** no primary matched Qwen3.8-27B abliterated-vs-base DFlash2/DSpark retraining A/B; no verified abliterated-aligned public sidecar; no EXL3 3.0-vs-4.0 bpw speculation sweep on SM70; no 32k/64k/128k per-method acceptance matrix on this target; no quality-neutral compressed/evicted KV result for this 128k target.
